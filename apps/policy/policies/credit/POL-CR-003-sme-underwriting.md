# SME Underwriting Standard

> Synthetic policy for evaluation only. Not issued by any real bank and not legal advice.

## 3.1 Business eligibility and trading history {#3.1}

Borrowers must be entities registered in Singapore with at least 2 full
years of trading history and annual revenue of at least S$200,000. Start-ups
between 12 and 24 months old qualify only for secured facilities capped at
50% of the standard limit with a full director guarantee. The following
sectors are excluded outright: gambling, cryptocurrency dealing, unlicensed
moneylending, and shell companies with no operating premises. Businesses
with a change of control in the past 12 months require 6 months of post-change
bank statements before any new facility is considered.

## 3.2 Financial analysis and DSCR application {#3.2}

Underwriting uses audited financials for exposures above S$1,000,000 and
management accounts plus 12 months bank statements below that threshold.
Revenue concentration above 40% from a single customer triggers a 15%
revenue haircut in the DSCR computation. Year-on-year revenue decline above
20% requires a written turnaround assessment countersigned by a manager.
The DSCR floors in POL-CR-002 section 2.2 apply; in addition, interest
coverage (EBIT over interest) must exceed 2.0x for unsecured SME facilities.
Negative operating cash flow in the latest year caps new unsecured exposure
at 3 months of average operating inflows.

## 3.3 Director guarantees and security {#3.3}

A joint and several personal guarantee from all directors holding 20% or
more equity is mandatory for unsecured SME facilities and for any business
younger than 3 years. Corporate guarantees from the ultimate holding company
are required where the borrower is a subsidiary and exposure exceeds
S$500,000. Guarantees must be supported by a statement of personal assets
and liabilities refreshed every 24 months. Capped guarantees are permitted
at no less than 150% of the facility amount. Release of a guarantor requires
full facility review and committee approval.

## 3.4 Ongoing monitoring and early warning {#3.4}

SME borrowers are graded quarterly on account conduct, cheque returns, and
utilisation patterns. Two cheque returns in 90 days, utilisation persistently
above 90% of limit, or overdues exceeding 30 days past due trigger an early
warning review within 14 days. Early warning outcomes are limited to: limit
freeze, conversion to reviewable tenor, additional collateral call, or exit
within 180 days. Exposures above S$750,000 require an annual site visit with
a written visit report filed to the credit file.
